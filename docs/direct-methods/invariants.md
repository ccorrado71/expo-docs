# Estimate of the structure invariants

*Command: `%invariants`*

Up to 10000 triplets relating reflections with normalized values |E| larger than a
given threshold (*strong triplets*) are stored for active use in the phasing.
*Psi-zero triplets*, relating two reflections with large |E| and one with |E| close to
zero, are also generated: they are used to define a special figure of merit (PSCOMB).

Negative quartets are generated by combining the psi-zero triplets in pairs; those with
cross-magnitudes smaller than a given threshold are estimated by their first
representation (Giacovazzo, 1976). These quartets give an important contribution to the
CPHASE figure of merit.

Active triplets can be estimated by the P3 distribution of Cochran (1955), where the
concentration parameter of the von Mises distribution is

$$
C = \frac{2\, |E_{\mathbf{h}} E_{\mathbf{k}} E_{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{k}}|}{\sqrt{N}}
$$

Triplets can also be estimated by their second representation, the **P10 formula**
(Cascarano *et al.*, 1984), whose von Mises concentration parameter is

$$
G = C\, (1 + q)
$$

where *q* is a function (positive or negative) of all the magnitudes in the second
representation of the triplet. The G values are rescaled on the C values and the
triplets are ranked in decreasing order of G. The top relationships are a better
selection of triplets with phase close to zero than the one obtained by ranking on C,
and are actively used in the phase determination.

Triplets with negative G are a good selection of relationships with phase close to
180°, used to compute a powerful figure of merit (CPHASE). Triplets with G close to zero
are expected to have phases widely spread around 90° or 270°, and are used to compute an
enantiomorph-sensitive figure of merit. A similar figure of merit is computed with
quartets estimated with a very small concentration parameter.

**By default, triplets are estimated by the P10 formula.**

When pseudotranslational symmetry information is available from the
[normalization](normalization.md), the parameter C of the Cochran distribution is
suitably modified: triplet phases are then no longer expected around zero and may lie
anywhere between 0 and 2π.

## Directives

The following directives of `%invariants` activate non-default procedures:

| Directive | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `gmin x` | positive triplets with G < *x* are not actively used. Default 0.6; accepted range 0.2 < *x* ≤ 1.0 (see Example 1) |
| `cochran` | use the P3 formula instead of the default P10 formula |
| `emax x` | maximum \|E\| of the weak reflections for the psi-zero search (up to `nrpsizero` reflections); *x* > 0.3 is not accepted |
| `emin x` | minimum \|E\| of the reflections used in the sigma-2 search; *x* < 1.0 is not accepted. Default: the minimum \|E\| of the strongest reflections selected by the normalization |
| `npsizero` | do not compute the psi-zero triplets (by default they are used) |
| `nrpsizero n` | number of weakest reflections for the psi-zero search (default: computed by the normalization) |
| `nrtriplets n` | number of strongest reflections for the sigma-2 search, down to \|E\| = 1.0 (default: computed by the normalization, *nstrong*); values smaller than *nstrong*/2 are not accepted |
| `nlarge n` | number of largest reflections for the psi-zero search (default: the number of strongest reflections used in the sigma-2 search); `nlarge` > `nrtriplets` is not accepted |
| `nquartets` | do not compute the negative quartets (by default they are used) |
| `numk n` | number of free vectors used by the P10 formula. Default: min(*natom*/*msym* + 30, 70), where *natom* is the number of atoms in the cell and *msym* the number of symmetry operators of the space group |

## Example

**Example 1.** Use of the `gmin` directive.

```text
%structure mes
%job MES - data from home diffractometer
%data
pattern mes.pow
cell 8.588 9.931 11.105 90.0 93.754 90.0
content C 24 N 4 O 20 S 4 H 52
spacegroup p 21/c
wavelength 1.5406
%extraction
%normal
%invariants
gmin 0.4
%continue
```
